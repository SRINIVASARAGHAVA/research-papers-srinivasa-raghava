Worked examples

Counting R(3) = 16
  1. List the four fractions. The thirds are 1/3 and 2/3. The halves are 1/2 and 3/2. Their subtotals add to 3. The subtotal from thirds must therefore be a multiple of both 1/3 and 1/2. Since 2 and 3 are coprime, that subtotal is an integer.
  2. Count the two groups. To make an integer subtotal j from thirds, the numbers of copies u and v satisfy u + 2v = 3j. For halves they satisfy u + 3v = 2j. In each case, choosing a nonnegative v up to the allowed maximum fixes u.

  3. Pair subtotals that add to 3. Each choice of thirds totaling j can be paired with every choice of halves totaling 3 − j. R(3) = 1 × 3 + 2 × 2 + 4 × 1 + 5 × 1 = 16.

Counting R(4) = 226
  1. Group the seven fractions. The thirds are 1/3, 2/3 and 4/3. The other parts are 1/4, 3/4, 1/2 and 3/2. The latter have denominators dividing 4. The thirds therefore have a subtotal that is a multiple of both 1/3 and 1/4, hence an integer.
  2. Count each group by subtotal. For thirds totaling j, count the nonnegative solutions of u + 2v + 4w = 3j. For example, at j = 2 there are four solutions with w = 0 and two with w = 1.

    The half parts total a multiple of 1/2. To reach the integer j, the quarter parts must therefore also total a multiple of 1/2. Write their subtotal as r/2, leaving (2j − r)/2 for the half parts. In quarter units the first count is ⌊2r/3⌋ + 1. In half units the second is ⌊(2j − r)/3⌋ + 1. Multiply the two counts and sum over r = 0, …, 2j. At j = 1 this gives 1 × 1 + 1 × 1 + 2 × 1 = 4.
